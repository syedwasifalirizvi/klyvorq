How This Calculator Works

Unlike naive calculators that simply divide your desired income by 2,000 hours, KLYVORQ accounts for the financial reality of running an independent business: taxes, overhead expenses, non-working downtime, and non-billable administrative hours.

1. Pre-Tax Revenue Target = Desired Net Income ÷ (1 - Tax Rate%)
2. Gross Annual Target = Pre-Tax Revenue Target + Annual Expenses
3. Working Weeks = 52 - Non-Working Weeks
4. Total Billable Hours = Working Weeks × Billable Hours Per Week
5. Recommended Hourly Rate = Gross Annual Target ÷ Total Billable Hours

Why Are Billable Hours Lower Than Working Hours?

If you work a 40-hour week, roughly 10 to 15 of those hours are taken up by non-billable overhead: responding to client inquiries, writing proposals, issuing invoices, marketing your portfolio, and managing technology.

If you calculate your hourly rate assuming 40 billable hours every week, you will invariably experience severe income shortfalls when client projects end or when administrative demands spike. Setting a conservative target of 20 to 25 billable hours per week creates a sustainable buffer.

Practical Real-World Scenario: Freelance Creative Consultant

Consider a freelance brand consultant targeting $75,000 take-home income. They incur $12,000 in software, hardware, and insurance expenses, face an effective 28% combined tax rate, take 4 weeks off annually (48 working weeks), and bill 25 client hours weekly.

  • Pre-tax income required: $75,000 ÷ (1 - 0.28) = $104,167
  • Gross revenue target with expenses: $104,167 + $12,000 = $116,167
  • Total billable annual hours: 48 weeks × 25 hours = 1,200 billable hours
  • Calculated hourly rate: $116,167 ÷ 1,200 = $96.81 / hour (approx. $775 / 8-hour day)

By charging $97/hour instead of an arbitrary $50/hour, the consultant fully covers business expenses and quarterly tax obligations without compromising personal savings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I quote clients hourly or on a fixed-project basis?
While calculating your internal hourly rate is crucial for understanding your cost of doing business, quoting clients on a fixed-fee or value-based basis is often superior. Use our Project Quote Calculator to bundle labor hours, scope buffers, and project margins into transparent project proposals.
What should I include in annual business expenses?
Include software subscriptions (Adobe Creative Cloud, GitHub, Figma), accounting and legal fees, computer hardware depreciation, web hosting, office supplies, health insurance premiums, retirement contributions, and co-working or desk rental fees.
How much should I set aside for taxes?
In most jurisdictions, self-employed professionals are responsible for both employee and employer portions of payroll/social insurance taxes, in addition to state or federal income taxes. A conservative estimate is generally 25% to 35% of pre-tax revenue, but you should always consult a local tax professional.
