Transparent Pricing Mathematics

Fixed-price projects fail when unanticipated feedback rounds and third-party expenses eat away at your hourly earnings. This tool computes an operational baseline and adds contingency and profit margins systematically.

1. Base Labor = Estimated Hours × Target Hourly Rate
2. Operational Cost Subtotal = Base Labor + Direct Expenses
3. Revision Allowance = Subtotal × Revision Buffer%
4. Total Cost = Operational Subtotal + Revision Allowance
5. Recommended Quote = Total Cost ÷ (1 - Profit Margin%)

Why You Must Add a Scope & Revision Buffer

Almost no creative or software project finishes exactly on the initially envisioned boundary. Clients request refinements, design tweaks, and content re-organization.

If you quote purely on base labor, every round of revision erodes your effective hourly earnings. A standard 15% to 25% revision allowance protects both you and the client: the client receives a thorough, polished outcome, while your business stays profitable.

Practical Real-World Scenario: Website Redesign Proposal

Suppose an agency is quoting a 5-page custom website redesign. They estimate 40 hours of production labor at $85/hour, with $350 in direct font and hosting expenses. They include a 15% revision buffer for client reviews and target a 20% business profit margin.

  • Base Labor: 40 hours × $85/hr = $3,400.00
  • Operational Subtotal: $3,400.00 + $350.00 = $3,750.00
  • 15% Revision Buffer: $3,750.00 × 15% = $562.50
  • Total Project Cost: $3,750.00 + $562.50 = $4,312.50
  • Final Recommended Quote: $4,312.50 ÷ (1 - 0.20) = $5,390.63 (~$5,391)
  • Net Business Profit: $5,390.63 × 20% = $1,078.13

The agency delivers a professional proposal that protects against unplanned meetings while guaranteeing healthy commercial profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the client does not use the revision buffer?
In a fixed-price arrangement, the quote is for delivering a specific scope of work, not billing actual clocked seconds. If you deliver efficiently with fewer revisions, the unused buffer becomes additional business profit.
Should I show this mathematical breakdown to my client?
Generally, no. Clients care about the total value and deliverables, not your internal contingency calculations. You should present the final Recommended Quote as your project fee, with a clear contract defining the number of included review rounds.
