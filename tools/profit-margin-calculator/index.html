The Difference Between Profit Margin and Markup

While people often use "margin" and "markup" interchangeably in casual conversation, they represent fundamentally different calculations. Confusing them is one of the quickest ways for a new business to lose money.

Gross Profit = Selling Price - Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
Profit Margin (%) = (Gross Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100
Markup (%) = (Gross Profit ÷ Cost) × 100
Target Price for Desired Margin = Cost ÷ (1 - Margin% ÷ 100)

Practical Real-World Scenario: E-Commerce Product Launch

Suppose an online boutique manufactures artisan leather bags at a direct production cost of $45.00 per unit. To cover customer acquisition, fulfillment, and business overhead, the owner requires a 55% gross profit margin.

  • Unit Cost: $45.00
  • Target Margin: 55.0%
  • Target Selling Price: $45.00 ÷ (1 - 0.55) = $100.00
  • Gross Profit per Unit: $100.00 - $45.00 = $55.00
  • Required Markup on Cost: ($55.00 ÷ $45.00) × 100 = 122.2%

Common Costly Mistake: If the owner had mistakenly applied a 55% markup to the $45 cost, they would have priced the bag at $69.75 ($45 × 1.55), resulting in only $24.75 profit and an actual margin of just 35.5%—leaving them unable to cover advertising costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can profit margin ever exceed 100%?
No. Profit margin is the ratio of profit to selling price. Since profit cannot exceed the total selling price (unless costs are negative, which is economically impossible in standard business), profit margin cannot exceed 100%. Markup, however, can easily exceed 100%, 200%, or even 1,000%.
What is a healthy profit margin for a small business?
Healthy gross margins vary significantly by industry. Supermarkets and retail commodities often operate on 2% to 15% margins with high turnover. Professional services and consulting often aim for 50% to 70% gross margins, while digital software products (SaaS) frequently exceed 80%.
